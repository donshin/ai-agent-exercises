## Were Things Stand

Adoption are allmost universal, atleast on paper, In a survey of 1,900 IT leaders publishd in April 2026 [OutSystems found](https://itwire.com/guest-articles/guest-research/agentic-ai-goes-mainstream-in-the-enterprise,-but-94-raise-concern-about-sprawl,-outsystems-research-finds.html) that 96% is useing AI agents in some capacity, and 97% are exploring system-wide agentic strategies and only 49% however calls there own capabilites advanced or expert. while 52% relies on a "human-on-the-loop" model meaning people supervises but dont approves every step. Financial services and technology firms reports the highest levels of production deployment, the same article cite a Gartner prediction that 40% of enterprise applications will includes task-specific agents by the end of 2026 which if true mean agents will been baked into software you already buys.

The plumbing are also maturing fast. in December 2025 Anthropic Block and OpenAI co-founded the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation, and [Anthropic donated](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-the-model-context-protocol-and-establishing-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation) the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to them. Block contributed goose, OpenAI contributing AGENTS.md. Anthropic report more then 10,000 active public MCP servers and 97 million+ monthly SDK downloads, translation their now is a shared standard for how agents connects to tools wich lowering the risk of betting on one vender

## The Part Nobody Want's To Here

Security are the biggest problem and its not hardly close. [Prompt injection](https://sdtimes.com/security/prompt-injection-tops-2026-owasp-genai-llm-top-ten-vulnerabilities/) rank number one on the 2026 OWASP GenAI/LLM Top Ten for the third year running Even more telling "excessive agency" (an agent with to much access or permission), climbed from eigth place to third. Steve Wilson of the OWASP GenAI Security Project put it plain, "Excessive agency rose from eighth to third because AI systems are no longer limited to generating text." His fix are boring but right: narrow permissions plus ongoing monitoring of behavior, and which most companys wont do.

Governance is lagging to; In the OutSystems data 94% of respondents worrys that AI sprawl are increasing complexity technical debt and security risk, yet just 12% has a centralized platform to manage it. Every team spinning up there own agents with there own credentials are how you ends up with a breach and no one whom can explane what happend

## What I Recomend

First start small, Pick one or two narrow workflows with clear success measures not a company wide rollout. Second keep a human in the loop for anything that spend money, change records or talk's to customer's. Third giving each agent the least access it need and log everthing it do. Forth stand up one central place to register and govern agents before sprawl get ahead of you; Finaly build on open standards like MCP so you can swapped vendors later without a rewrite

If we does this right agents will earn there keep, if we skips it we be explaning an incident to the board. Im happy to walk through any of this at our next meeting just let me no
